## Text Elements
Irony - When something happens that is the opposite of what people expect.
Example: Mr. Kelada is called "Mr. Know-All" because people think he is annoying and talks too much. But in the end, he pretends to be wrong and loses a bet to protect Mrs. Ramsay's secret. The biggest "know-all" looks like a fool to save a woman's honor.

Personification - Giving human actions or feelings to an object or thing.
Example: The narrator says his cabin porthole had "darkness staring in" from the ocean outside, as if the darkness had eyes and was looking right at him.

Simile - Comparing two things using the words "like" or "as".
Example: The narrator describes Mr. Kelada's long black hair as "sleek and curly like an Italian's" (or his smile flashing like a row of white teeth).

Symbolism - An object that has a deeper meaning or represents a big idea.
The pearl necklace is the main symbol. To Mr. Ramsay, it looks like cheap glass jewelry. But in reality, it is very expensive and represents Mrs. Ramsay's hidden secret and unfaithfulness. It also tests Mr. Kelada's true character. ^ozdPFVy3
